Two conventions of the round, from the probe (`01`, K6 and K9):

- **The depth is a constant under `#pragma unroll 1`.** A row loop with a
  compile-time trip count is unrolled and its loads hoisted, so a batch
  constant means nothing without the pragma. Every batch loop of this
  page carries it, and the offline disassembly's `s_waitcnt vmcnt`
  sequence is the check that the loads in flight are the constant's.
- **A wave-load is one contiguous KB.** Lane l reads the 16-byte chunk
  `8 l + 512 i` (elements) of a 2,048-element row, chunk `l + 64 j` of a
  1,408-element row, one whole row of 512 elements, or eight or sixteen
  lanes of a row's 128- or 256-byte segment; never a lane-contiguous
  slice.

## Part 1: the GEMV linear

### L1. The tile kernel (K1, K5 to K9 of `01`)

**Direction.** One kernel for every dense linear at batch 1: R rows of a
`[N, K]` BF16 weight against one `[K]` row, FP32 accumulation, BF16 store,
with the input norm, the residual add and the padded tail as template
flags. It replaces the CK tile whose K loop keeps one step in flight.

**Core approach.**

- `linear_gemv_mi300_task_impl<T, K, NORM, RESIDUAL>(x, w_norm, W,
  residual, out, rows, o_stride, eps)`: `rows` is this task's row count
  (38, 32 or 256), the weight pointer already offset to its rows by the
  runtime, `out` to its columns, `o_stride` the full N.
- Wave w owns rows `w * RPW .. min((w + 1) * RPW, rows) - 1` with
  `RPW = ceil(rows / 4)` (10, 10, 9, 9 for 38); it walks them in batches
  of `GEMV_BATCH` (8) rows under `#pragma unroll 1`. A row is four
  16-byte loads per lane at chunks `8 l + 512 i` through `load16_from`
  (`StreamSrc`, the `sc1 nt` policy of the linears; plain loads without
  `MLA_NT_STREAMS`). A batch is 32 loads per lane, 128 KB per CU in
  flight, 158 VGPRs by the probe.
- The x slice: 32 FP32 values per lane at the same chunks, loaded once.
  NORM: `rmsnorm_row` runs first with `out = nullptr` (the helper gains
  the null guard; today it stores unconditionally) and `out_s` the LDS
  row, and the slice is read from LDS; plain: four `load8` from x.
- The first batch's loads, and the residual's 16-byte chunk of the task's
  columns for RESIDUAL, are issued before the prologue, so the norm's
  round trip and reductions run under the batch's latency.
- The multiply: per row per lane, 32 conversions and 32 `v_fmac_f32` in
  ascending k within the lane's four runs of eight; then the halving
  butterfly over the batch's rows: three steps in which lane l exchanges
  half of its live row sums with lane `l ^ 32`, `l ^ 16`, `l ^ 8` (4, 2
  and 1 values) and adds, then three single-value steps (`l ^ 4`, `l ^ 2`,
  `l ^ 1`): 10 shuffles for eight rows against 48 for eight wave sums,
  leaving lane l < 8 with row `r0 + l`'s sum.
- The epilogue: lane l < 8 adds the residual value in FP32 (RESIDUAL),
  rounds with `bf16r` and stores `out[r0 + l]`; rows past `rows` and
  columns past N are masked (the tail); `nt_store` as the stock epilogue.
- The head's 256 rows are 64 per wave, eight batches, the norm once.
- Numerics: the products are exact; the accumulation order is a lane's
  chain of 32 and the butterfly's tree, against the MFMA's order today,
  within the tolerance the boundary compare applies to that order.

**Files.** `fleet/tasks/mi300/linear_gemv_mi300.cuh` (new);
`mla_common_mi300.cuh` (the null guard in `rmsnorm_row`; a
`butterfly_sum<ROWS>` helper beside `wave_sum`); `check_syntax.sh` (the
three instantiations: `<bf16, 2048, true, false>`, `<bf16, 2048, false,
true>`, `<bf16, 2048, false, false>`).

**Checks here.** `check_syntax.sh`; the suite rows of L1b; the offline
variant of L1c.

## Part 2: the router and the merge

### N1. The router, one level deeper (R1, R2, R3, R4, R6)

**Direction.** The same router, its four round trips made two, its norm
hidden under the first batch, its loads coalesced and streaming, its
writes spread over the lanes. The math and every output unchanged.

**Core approach.**

- The GEMV loop restructured: wave w's 16 experts in two batches of
  `ROUTER_BATCH` (8) rows under `#pragma unroll 1`; a row's four chunks
  at `8 lane + 512 i` through `load16_from` on a `StreamSrc` of the gate

  `mask[s]`, the log), lane 0 the count; the ids and weights reach the

- `k_moe_router` in the suite unchanged in interface; `ROUTER_BATCH` and
  `ROUTER_STRIDED` as compile-time switches for the `ktime` A/B (three

### N2. The router in four tasks (R5)

**Direction.** The GEMV over four tasks of 16 experts, one batch each,
and the last-arriving task routes.

**Core approach.**

- `moe_router_mi300_task_impl` gains `SPLIT` (1 or 4) and a `part`

  task's threads `__builtin_amdgcn_fence(__ATOMIC_ACQUIRE, "agent")`, wave

### N3. The merge, one level deeper (M1, M2, M3)

**Direction.** The same merge, its four round trips made about two: the
lse read inside the partials batch, the `W_uv` rows issued before and
under the partials, every wave-load a full KB, the o values in registers.

**Core approach.**

- Phase order: (a) issue `W_uv` batch 0 (wave w's rows `32 w .. 32 w +
  15`, one 1 KB wave-load per row, 16 loads per lane); (b) issue the
  partials batch: thread (q, s) loads for its rows j = s, s + 4, ... the
  floats `4 q .. 4 q + 3` and `256 + 4 q .. 256 + 4 q + 3` (two loads
  per row, `ROWS_IN_FLIGHT = 9`) and, for q = 0, the lse word at column
  512 (one more load per row); (c) when the lse words arrive, the q = 0
  threads write `lse_s[j]`, one barrier, every thread computes M, the
  33 weights and their total from `lse_s` (the same floats as today);
  (d) the FMAs in ascending j into eight accumulators (the two runs of
  four columns), `red_s` and the barrier, `o_s[c] = bf16r(sum * inv_tot)`
  and the barrier, as today; (e) issue `W_uv` batch 1 (rows `32 w + 16
  ..`) as soon as the partials registers are free, before (d)'s
  reductions; (f) the lane's eight o values from `o_s[8 lane ..]`; the
  FMAs of batch 0 (eight per row per lane), the butterfly over its 16
  rows, lanes 0 to 15 store `attn[v0 + lane]` as BF16 (32 contiguous
  bytes); the same for batch 1.
- Registers: at most about 72 (partials) + 9 (lse) + 64 (`W_uv` batch)
  + accumulators, or 64 + 64 + 8 during (e) and (f): under 180.
- `ROWS_IN_FLIGHT` follows `n_splits` (9 for 33, a `static_assert`
  against the suite's `N_SPLITS`); `MERGE_W_BATCH` 16; `pf.sh` is
  unchanged (it toggles the attention kernel only).

**Files.** `mla_merge_uv_mi300.cuh`, `mla_common_mi300.cuh` (the
butterfly), `check_syntax.sh` (the same signature).

**Checks here.** `check_syntax.sh`; the suite's `mla_merge_uv` rows dry
run (the o partials within FP32 noise of `merge_partials`, `attn` within
tolerance); the offline `k_mla_merge_uv` line (at most 200 VGPRs) and its
wait sequence from `dev_kt.s`; `ktime`.

**Time box.** 5 hours. **On the VM.** H0, H3.

### N4. The merge as regular tasks (M6, M4)

**Direction.** The merge as 16 or 32 regular tasks, whole-tensor imaps,
the head and the half from the task index: the shape M5 builds on.

**Core approach.** `mla_merge_uv_mi300_task_impl` gains `HALVES` (1 or 2)

**Time box.** 3 hours. **On the VM.** H4.

### N5. The merge with o_proj folded in (M5)

**Direction.** The o_proj operator removed: each merge task multiplies
its head's (or half head's) `attn` values by its slice of `W_o` into a
partial vector, and the last-arriving task sums the partials in fixed
order with the residual into `x_res`.

**Core approach.**

- `mla_merge_oproj_mi300.cuh` (new; `TASK_MLA_MERGE_OPROJ_MI300 = 202`,
  regular, in the `expert_offset` list): N3's and N4's merge to the
  point where the task's 64 (or 128) `attn` values are BF16-rounded in
  LDS and stored to `attn` (the boundary keeps its row); then phase 4:
  the task's `W_o` slice is columns `64 t .. 64 t + 63` of every row of
  `W_o [2048, 2048]` (128 contiguous bytes per row, 128-byte aligned):
  eight lanes per row, eight rows per wave-load, wave w rows `8 w + 32
  b + r`, 64 loads per lane in two batches of 32 under `#pragma unroll
  1`; a lane's product is its eight `attn` values (registers) against
  its chunk; the eight lanes of a row reduce by three xor steps (1, 2,
  4); the lane at the row's head writes `workspace[t][n]` (FP32); then
  a barrier, all threads `__builtin_amdgcn_fence(__ATOMIC_RELEASE,
  "agent")`, thread 0 `atomicAdd(counter, 1)` and the broadcast of
  `old == 31`; the last task: the acquire fence, thread t loads
  `workspace[0..31][8 t .. 8 t + 7]` (two batches of 32 loads) and sums
  them in order 0 to 31, adds `x_res[8 t ..]` in FP32, rounds and stores
  `x_res[8 t ..]` (16 bytes), thread 0 stores `counter = 0`.
- The registration: inputs `partials, W_uv, W_o, x_res, counter`, outputs
  `x_res, attn, workspace [32, 2048]` FP32 (in-place `x_res` as
  `linear_with_residual` does), params `[split, n_splits, halves]`, grid
  `(32, 1, 1)`, every imap whole. `graph_plan.py --merge-oproj` allocates
  `oproj_ws` and `oproj_counter`, replaces `L{l}.mla_merge_uv` and
  `L{l}.o_proj` by one call labelled `L{l}.o_proj` (so `--stop-after` and
  the compare's `x_res` boundary keep their key; the `attn` boundary's
  last writer is the same operator), for every layer; the counts (one
  operator and 64 - 32 tasks fewer per layer); `numpy_ref` unchanged.
- Numerics: each output is 32 partial sums of 64 terms (a lane's chain of
  eight and the row's three-step tree over its eight lanes), summed in
  fixed order, the residual added, rounded: deterministic, a different
  FP32 order from the CK MFMA's, the same class as the GEMV's.

**Files.** `mla_merge_oproj_mi300.cuh`, `new_tasks.patch` (the type, the
list, the registration, the dispatcher case), `build_graph.py`,
`graph_plan.py` (the flag, the tensors, the labels), `harness/compare.py`

counter; `x_res` against `numpy_ref` merge then o_proj with the residual
within the linear tolerance, `attn` bit-exact against the merge row),
`mk_tu.cu`, `check_syntax.sh`.

**Checks here.** `check_syntax.sh`; the suite row's dry run; the dry run's
counts and the compare's boundary list; the patch regenerates; the
offline variant's registers (the three phases must not multiply the live
registers: at most 200).

**Time box.** 8 hours. **On the VM.** H5.
